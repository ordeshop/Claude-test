# Hook library (talking-head spoken hooks + faceless visual/text hooks)

There are two hook toolkits here. Use the **spoken** formulas for talking-head format, and the
**faceless** visual/text hooks for the satisfying/ASMR format. Always deliver three options.

---

## Part 1 — Talking-head spoken hooks

The hook decides the ad. Aim for 10–14 words, landing the pattern interrupt before the
2-second mark, spoken AND on-screen. Always deliver three, on different formulas, so the
user can A/B test the opener with the same body.

## Formulas + fill-in patterns

**Pattern interrupt / shock** — something that violates expectation.
- "This $[low price] [thing] replaced my entire [expensive category]."
- "I stopped [common habit] and my [outcome] completely changed."
- Example: "This $6 serum replaced my $200 skincare shelf."

**Curiosity gap** — open a loop the brain can't leave unfinished (Zeigarnik effect).
- "Nobody tells you this about [topic]."
- "I wish someone told me this before I [action]."
- Example: "Nobody tells you why your [product category] keeps failing."

**Problem-solution** — name a sharp pain, promise the fix.
- "If your [thing] keeps [problem], you're making this one mistake."
- "Struggling with [pain]? Watch this before you buy anything else."

**POV / contrarian** — identity-based; viewers agree (validation) or disagree (engagement).
- "POV: you finally stopped [pain behavior]."
- "Unpopular opinion: [category norm] is a waste of money."

**Confession / controversy** — best for apps and software; overcomes skepticism.
- "I did NOT expect an app to actually [benefit]."
- "I was so wrong about [category]."

**Story / narrative** — start mid-action with tension.
- "So I almost returned this… until day three."
- "The day I wore this, four people asked where I got it."

**Number / listicle** — concrete and scannable.
- "Three reasons I'll never go back to [old solution]."

**Before/after visual-first** — show the transformation first, explain after. Strong for
beauty, fitness, home, food. The visual IS the hook; the first spoken words come a beat later.

## Verbal + visual pairing

The best-performing UGC pairs a verbal hook with a **visual** hook in the same 3 seconds:
- genuine surprise / excitement / confusion on the creator's face,
- or the end result shown first (the glow-up, the clean room, the packed suitcase),
- or an unexpected object/gesture that doesn't match the setting.

Always note the visual hook alongside the verbal one — on TikTok the thumbnail-frame and
motion do as much work as the words.

## Anti-patterns (avoid)

- Corporate openers: "We're excited to introduce…" — instant scroll.
- Vague rhetorical questions: "Tired of bad skincare?" — too generic; be specific and personal.
- Burying the hook behind a logo intro or slow pan. The pattern interrupt must be frame one.

## Part 2 — Faceless visual + text hooks

In a faceless video the hook is what the viewer **sees and hears** first, plus a short text overlay
(5–8 words). The visual does most of the work; the text just frames it. Give three on different approaches.

**Reveal-first** — open on the most satisfying end state, then rewind to how it got there.
- Visual: the perfectly full shelf / the finished result / the gadget mid-"wow" moment.
- Text: "wait for it 🫧" · "the restock is so satisfying" · "watch how this ends"

**"For those who…" / "POV:"** — name the viewer or the use-case over an aesthetic setup.
- Text: "POV: your snack drawer is finally organized" · "for those who love a clean fridge" ·
  "for people who overthink desk setups"

**Curiosity / "watch till the end"** — promise a payoff or an unexpected feature.
- Text: "mini but powerful ✨ watch till the end" · "you're using this wrong" ·
  "the last step changes everything"

**Problem-state cold open** — open on the mess/empty/annoyance the product resolves.
- Visual: the chaotic drawer, the empty shelf, the tangled cables.
- Text: "this was stressing me out" · "not anymore"

### Faceless hook tips

- The first frame must be *visually* interesting on its own (thumbnail test) — motion or a clean reveal,
  never a static logo.
- Sound is part of the hook: the first crinkle/pour/click should hit within the first second.
- Keep text to one short line; let the visual breathe.
- Anti-patterns: a slow pan-in before anything happens; text that explains what we can already see;
  starting on the packaging/brand instead of the satisfying action.
